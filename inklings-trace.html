INKLINGS · DEV BENCH

Trace Bench — the nib engine & the stroke scorer

M1 + M2 of the Scriptorium (docs/inklings-typography.md). Trace the letter stroke by stroke; the grade is per-stroke fidelity against the authored skeleton, and the ink is a nib stamped along your path — which is the whole claim of §3: stroke contrast is a record of the tool, so one skeleton plus a pen gives you a typeface. Glyphs come from Wordshape's authored alphabet (data/wordshape-alphabet.json if present, else sliced live out of wordshape-draw.html so the two can't drift).

M2 adds the faces. data/nibs.json and data/typefaces.json now hold the pens and the pruned Vox-ATypI tree, and fonts/ holds one OFL face per classification leaf — so the specimen you compare your trace against is a genuine typeface, not our approximation. Pick a face, pick the pen you think wrote it, and the three cells below the pad are §3's whole argument side by side: the real font, the skeleton in that face's own nib, and your hand.

· a–z picks a letter · 1–9 picks a nib

Press and drag to write one stroke at a time, in the order a hand would. The numbered dot is where the next stroke starts. A stroke that starts too far off, or runs backwards, is refused and shows you the ghost — that's §5.2's gates, not a difficulty setting.
